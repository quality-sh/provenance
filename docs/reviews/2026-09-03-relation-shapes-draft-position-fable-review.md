---

**VERDICT: ACCEPT WITH AMENDMENTS.** The direction (no canonical edge; owner field or action record; reverse lookups derived; act-relations keep their own families) is right and the evidence supports it. The table is wrong in five of nine rows, the migration order is backwards, and the draft is silent on the one thing that actually breaks 0.1 users (schema version). Amendments, in product words:

- A1. `produces` becomes two optional lists on the rule: `requirement_ids`, `resolution_ids`. `resolves` becomes a list `requirement_ids` on the resolution. Not singular fields.
- A2. `supersedes` is a list on the newer record (`supersedes: [...]`); `superseded_by` is derived. Requirement gains it; Source and Resolution keep `superseded_by` readable one release.
- A3. `spawns` is an optional `spawned_by` field on the spawned requirement, not a list on the resolution.
- A4. `refines_into` is an optional `refines` field on the child. Drop "required" and "root"; a root is a requirement with no `refines`.
- A5. `needs` backfill = `needs ∪ resolves`, with a printed report of the two disagreeing pairs.
- A6. `contradicts` is a question, not a review. Its `links` name both requirements; its `resolution_id` settles it. Do not use the word review for it.
- A7. Bump `schema_version` on requirements, resolutions, and rules when the new fields land, or state in the position that a 0.1/0.2 binary writing those shards will silently strip them.
- A8. Rule 3 reads: "reverse lookups are derived and never stored; the projection materializes them for served reads, `RecordFront` derives them for canonical readers (check, gaps, wiki, merge)."
- A9. Migration order: add fields + backfill + write both → flip readers to fields under the byte-parity harness → stop writing edges → delete shard. "Stop double writes" cannot be step one.
- A10. Name the declaration mechanism: a field attribute in `provenance-macros` that generates the `RelationKind` data, or say plainly it is a table plus a struct-walking test.

## Findings, most severe first

1. **Singular fields lose 18 live rows.** 14 rules have more than one requirement producer (`rule_prov_edge_endpoint_table`, `rule_sdk_protocol_handshake`, ...), 4 rules have more than one resolution producer, and `res_dispositions_sole_authority` resolves 3 requirements. The table's "a required requirement field on the rule, plus an optional resolution field" and "a required field on the resolution" cannot hold them. Probe over `.provenance/state/edges/edges-00.jsonl`.
2. **Mixed-version data loss, unaddressed.** `crates/provenance-store/src/state_store/readers.rs:60-75`: every write is a read first and "writes the whole shard back, dropping any unrecognised field on the way out"; only a later `schema_version` is refused at the door. v0.1.0 (2026-08-23) and v0.2.1 (2026-08-25) both ship this path. A teammate on 0.2.1 running `requirements fog set` on a migrated shard deletes every `refines`/`requirement_ids` in that file. The draft never mentions schema version; the record-owned artifact says "stays at 1". That is the wrong call for the three shards that gain load-bearing fields.
3. **`supersedes` owner is wrong and unwritable.** `Requirement` has no `superseded_by` (`crates/provenance-core/src/model/artifacts.rs:284-318`), so "the existing superseded_by field on the older record" does not exist for the one kind that has a `supersedes` edge type. The only writer that sets `superseded_by` sets it on the record being created (`writers.rs:22,43`; `rule_writers.rs:347,376`); no command edits the older record. Live state disagrees both ways: `res_rule_is_the_function` status `superseded`, no `superseded_by`; `res_convex_chosen_as_backend_over_custom_web` has `superseded_by`, status `draft`. The record authored at supersession time is the successor.
4. **`spawns` owner contradicts rule 1.** `docs/shaping.md:110-122`: the resolution lands, then it "spawns a Requirement"; the requirement is the line being authored, and there is no `resolutions update`. The one live row (`res_ste_local_dictionary_acquisition -> req_ste_local_dictionary_acquisition`) is exactly that order. Putting a list on the resolution edits a reviewed, approved record.
5. **"Required parent, absent only on a root" is vacuous.** 49 of 68 requirements have no parent; `TypedRequirementInput` (`crates/provenance-core/src/protocol/typed_spec.rs:66-75`) has no parent slot, so every `sdk apply` requirement is a root forever. Nothing can enforce "required".
6. **`needs` is not a mirror.** Two resolutions carry `needs` from a requirement they do not `resolves` (`res_sdk_engine_from_package_manager`, `res_typed_facade_owns_construction`). "Dropped; it mirrors resolves" loses two pairs.
7. **`contradicts` as "a review" collides with ADR 0007.** `RequirementReview` (`crates/provenance-core/src/model/integrations.rs:153-167`) is rule-scoped, machine-raised by `sdk apply`, carries `field/before/after`, no actor, no evidence, cleared by a verification run (`requirement_reviews.rs:90-112`). It cannot name two requirements. See Q2.
8. **Migration order in the draft breaks every reader.** Readers of `produces`/`resolves` edges that run on canonical state, not the projection: `cache/gaps/graph_query.rs:331-485`, `cache/gaps/contradiction.rs:10-58`, `cache/gaps/dangling.rs:645-670`, `cache/health.rs:73-99,181-210`, `cache/prime.rs:126-137`, `cache/impact.rs:44-60`, `cache/traceability.rs:39-90`, `operations/plan.rs:131`, `state_store/requirement_reviews.rs:132-147`, `typed_specs/adoption/relationships.rs:59-91`, `wiki/assemble/{context,traversal,discovery}.rs`, `operations/queries/walk.rs:11-19`. "Stop double writes" first leaves all of them reading a shard that stops growing.
9. **`RecordFront` has zero production consumers.** `grep RecordFront|related_nodes` outside `provenance-core` returns nothing. The draft leans on W4 for reverse lookups; W4 is dormant. The flip in A9 step two is where it earns its keep.
10. **Wire and export surfaces the draft undercounts.** `EdgeType` is on the wire in `NeighborsRequest.edge_types`, `TraceRequest.edge_types`, `Neighbor.edge_type` (`packages/provenance/src/protocol.ts:186-195,246-257`; `core/protocol/query.rs:78,97`). `edges` is a fixed family of Graph reference v1 under `deny_unknown_fields` (`graph_reference/projection.rs:31,47`; `docs/cli.md:384`) and of export/import (`handlers/export.rs:27`; `handlers/import/scope_writer.rs:111-132`). Retiring it is protocol 6 and graph reference v2 (every scope's digest moves), or `edges` stays as a derived view named by `RelationKind::name` (additive, protocol 5) and import maps it to fields with the same backfill.
11. **`prime` misses the 3 field-only citations today** (`cache/prime.rs:134-137` reads `References` edges only). The migration fixes this silently; the differential harness will flag it as drift. Say so up front.
12. **`depends_on` may be the wrong kind.** `docs/shaping.md:62` wires "blocking edges" between questions; the edge type is declared requirement→requirement with 0 rows. Drop it or put `blocked_by` on questions.

## Answers

**1. Ownership.** references: requirement (correct; the clause is only there). refines_into: child, single optional `refines` (each of 19 children has one parent; wiki `traversal.rs:11-18,94-99` and `discovery.rs:333-341` need the reverse scan for children and siblings). depends_on: dependent requirement is right if the kind survives; see finding 12. supersedes: the newer record; when the newer record is immutable (proposal card) ADR 0001 already routes it through the disposition (`ideation/lifecycle.rs:102`), which is rule 2 exactly; when the older record is the one being edited there is no writer today, so nothing is lost by refusing that path. spawns: the spawned requirement (`spawned_by`); a resolution approved after the requirement exists is `resolves`, not `spawns`. produces when the rule precedes its resolution: `rules create --resolution-id` requires the resolution to exist (`rule_writers.rs:439-446`), and there is no `rules update`, so today the only after-the-fact path is `edges create`; under the draft the rule owns `resolution_ids` and needs an update command. Root requirement: a requirement with no `refines`; `domain_id` is not a root marker (67/68 have one, children included).

**2. Contradicts.** A resolution is the only graph record with actor, date, evidence (`made_by`, `approved_by/at`, `inputs`) that can name two requirements once `requirement_ids` is a list, and `contradiction.rs:49-57` already treats a shared resolution as settling the pair. But the finding precedes the settlement, and a draft shared resolution would count as settled (`is_resolved` never checks status). A question fits the finding: `links` name both requirements (`shaping.rs:162`), `resolution_id` settles it, the thread carries actor and date, and the frontier already lists open questions beside contradiction pairs (`shaping.md:70-72`). Cost: a question requires `topic_id` and `requirement_id` (`shaping.rs:146-149`). The ADR 0007 review is a different thing (finding 7). If the owner wants a new kind, it needs a new word; do not reuse review.

**3. What breaks.** Wire: finding 10. Declaration: `RequirementDeclaration.sources` and `RuleDeclaration.requirement(s)` map onto `source_refs` and `requirement_ids` directly; `relationships.rs:8-37` (edge write), `relationships.rs:68-145` (stale edge delete), `adoption/relationships.rs` (ADR 0008 equality over edges), `requirement_reviews.rs:132-147` and `plan.rs:131` (review fan-out over `produces`) all move to field comparison. The wire allows a rule with no requirement (`typed_spec.rs:85-88`, both optional) though the fluent surface nests rules under requirements (`fluent-spec.ts:284-296`); "required" on the rule contradicts the wire. A declaration cannot express `refines`, `supersedes`, `spawned_by`, `resolution_ids`, or a contradiction; it needs at least `requirements[].refines`. Merge: `ShardFamily` gains Resolutions (typed deserialize) and loses Edges (`merge/validation.rs:34-47,102-113`); it can never check that an id exists. Gaps: the seven joins in `graph_query.rs` become fk/embedded scans like `front.rs:376-446`; `OrphanRule`/`OrphanResolution` stay while the fields are optional; `UnresolvedContradictsPair` folds into `OpenQuestion` under A6; `add_edge_refs` becomes per-field dangling checks. Wiki, trace, impact, traceability: all walk edge rows on canonical state (finding 8). W4: `RelationKind` keeps its 21 names; nine `EdgeRow` derivations become `FkField`/`EmbeddedCollection`; `Needs` and `Contradicts` variants go; `same_fact_as`/`drop_duality_echoes` (`front.rs:94-118`) are deleted. Scanner and coverage: nothing reads or writes edges (grep of `crates/provenance-scanner` and `handlers/coverage*` is empty). W3/W5: the plan serves `neighbors`/`trace` off `idx_edges_scope_type_from/to` (plan W3 §2; `migrations/005`); the SQLite `edges` table can stay, loaded per scope from fields instead of `list_edges()` (`materialize/graph_records.rs:182-190`); `ProjectionFamily::Edges` stops being the one global family (`projection_families.rs:93-96`), which removes the "edges global" special case ADR 0009 carried. That is a change to the loader and the byte domain, not to cursors. The differential gate's old executors (`walk.rs`) must run against the derived table, so land the derivation while the shard still exists.

**4. Migration.** Lossless per type given A1–A5: references (76 ⊂ 79 field pairs), refines_into (19, one parent each), depends_on/supersedes (0), resolves (95 → lists), produces (323 → lists), spawns (1). Not lossless: needs (finding 6) and contradicts (the row has no actor or date; a question needs a topic). No edge points at a missing or retired record; all 612 are scope `default`. A newer binary opening a 0.1 repository: `read_edge_shards` (`readers.rs:357-369`) must keep reading the shard until the delete step, and import of a v1 export must map `edges` to fields. Schema: A7. Staging: A9; steps one and two leave 0.1 binaries fully working; at step three an old binary's gap report goes red (orphan rules everywhere) but destroys nothing; the destructive path is an old binary writing a shard, which is what the schema bump refuses.

**5. Reverse lookups without the projection.** Yes, everything in finding 8 runs on canonical records under the publication lock; the plan itself records zero production readers of `provenance.db` (W2 reader audit). `check` needs no reverse lookup (`check/edges.rs` becomes per-field existence checks). Gaps, health, wiki, prime, impact, traceability, plan, review fan-out all need "who names X"; the in-memory answer is `RecordFront::fk/embedded` In-direction scans. Materialization in SQLite is not enough; A8.

**6. The declaration.** Closest today: `RelationKind` (`relations.rs:184-400`: name, derivation, from/to types), `ProjectionFamily` (`projection_families.rs:23-66`), `ShardFamily`, `TypedDeclarationKind`, and the `RecordFront` match arms (`front.rs:170-323`). Every one is a hand-written match with no tie to a struct field; the fable review's finding 8 and 1(b) and the GLM review lines 190-224 say what that cost W2. Here the risk lands as six places per new reference field: struct field, `RelationKind` variant, `RecordFront` arm, `SqlFront` fragment, `ShardFamily`, check arm. The only compile-time tie available is a derive attribute on the field (`provenance-macros` already hosts `#[rule]`); a table plus a serde-walking completeness test is the honest fallback. Pick one (A10).

**7. SaaS.** Holds for citations (owner row + child table `requirement_source_refs`, keyed by owner), dependencies (dependent row), and contradictions (a question row). Peer relations do not force a global relation table; multi-valued lists with metadata become owner-keyed child tables, which is a relation table by shape but not an edge. After-the-fact assertions are why A2 and A3 matter: successor-owned `supersedes` and requirement-owned `spawned_by` mean a later writer edits only its own row. The one thing fields cannot hold is a cross-scope reference (the edges shard is global with `scope_id`; fields live in one scope's shard); no such row exists today.

**8. Do not water down.** No canonical edge. Act-relations stay their own families with `declared_by`/`retired` (edges_first wanted them under an edge envelope; refuse that). One owner per fact; reverse lookups derived. The citation clause stays on the requirement. `needs` goes. The global hot shard (45 of 67 state commits) goes, which also simplifies W2/ADR 0009. Drop the "Rust's ownership model" rationale; it is rhetoric and invites the wrong argument.
