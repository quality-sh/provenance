About this proposal — stance, tradeoffs, what is agreed vs invented

Stance

The detachment Ben described is about provenance of structure: claims appear without their words. This workbench keeps the source addressable and the description citable in both directions — every record shows which passage it stands on, and every passage shows which records stand on it.

Hypothesis

Precise citation, using the existing source-plus-clause reference, is the cheapest strong answer to “where did this come from”. The bridge gesture (choose one side, see the other) keeps orientation without a graph to maintain.

Tradeoffs

Demands well-segmented sources; a long transcript crowds the pane. The two-pane split leaves less room for either side, and relations beyond citation (refines, spawns) stay in the compressed outline rather than a full picture. General passage anchoring beyond free-text clauses is deliberately out of scope — separate future shaping work.

Agreed vs invented

Agreed: both requirements, both decisions, the recorded question, the source, and the two verbatim passages with their locators; the detachment passage appears as an honest paraphrase. Invented: the sub-requirement, the rule, sample discussion lines, and the editing question.

Citation

Citation = source + free-text clause, as stored today. Finer passage anchoring is separate future shaping work.
