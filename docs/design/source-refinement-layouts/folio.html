About this proposal — stance, tradeoffs, what is agreed vs invented

Stance

The shared view is a document you keep, not a screen you visit. One requirement is the page subject; sub-requirements, candidate rules, decisions, open questions and attached sources are its numbered, collapsible sections. The discussion sits beside the page, bound to whichever record is selected.

Hypothesis

Detachment comes from structure arriving without a place. Giving every landed record an address in a standing page lets the person point at a section and argue with it — the outline itself becomes an agenda for the conversation.

Tradeoffs

Collapse hides the very evolution the requirement asks to expose; the outline must carry the load when sections are closed. Nesting also favours tree-shaped systems — cross-links between distant records (a rule grounding itself in a source passage) need the citation layer to stay honest.

Agreed vs invented

Agreed, recorded in the graph: the two requirements, the two approved decisions, the page-form open question, the source and its two verbatim passages, the detachment and “structure” citations. Invented here: the sub-requirement, the rule, both sample threads, and the editing-behaviour question. Illustrative items are marked in place.

Citation

Citation = source + free-text clause, as stored today. Finer passage anchoring is separate future shaping work.
